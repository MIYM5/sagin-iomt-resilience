Three observations follow. First, priority matters mainly under normal operation. With continuous coverage, moving from FIFO to hub priority lowers ECG misses from 0.48\% to 0.37\%, and adding relay priority lowers them further to 0.07\%; alarm misses fall from 0.60\% to 0.04\%. Hence, most of the gain comes from the relay, which is where alarms and ECG messages queue behind record fragments. Second, when gaps or jamming dominate, priority changes the miss rates by at most about 1 percentage point, because no scheduling order can deliver traffic over a link that is absent. Third, sleep costs nothing in timeliness: P4 and P3F have identical miss rates in every scenario, so P4's energy saving in Table~\ref{tab:main} comes free. Moreover, P3F at 10\,dBm and P1 at 23\,dBm give identical results without an attack, which confirms that transmit power alone does not improve timeliness when the hub link is healthy.
